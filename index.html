THE BUSINESS SIDE

The back office you don't have a team for.

Most independent artists handle the contracts, splits, invoices and pitches alone. Vault Clerk keeps that paperwork in order, so you spend less time on admin and more on the music. You don't need expensive lessons, and you don't have to hand your business to someone you hardly know.

More time for the music

Drop new files in your Inbox and Vault Clerk suggests where each one belongs. Search inside your contracts, PDFs and scans by the words in them. Paste a brief and it builds the paperwork. You check it and say yes.

Protect your work and your money

Split sheets, PRO and IPI numbers, quotes and invoices sit in one vault. Projects with unsigned splits are marked DARK and share previews only, so you see what still needs signing before you pitch.

Show up ready to negotiate

18 templates come with the app and fill in your details, so you start from your own paperwork. For a sync brief, gig offer or collab, Request puts the deal brief, quote, rights summary and the right audio in one package.

Vault Clerk does the paperwork. It doesn't give advice.

Vault Clerk isn't a lawyer, an accountant or a financial adviser. Nothing in the app or on this page is legal, tax or financial advice, and the templates are starting points. Before you sign a contract or make a money decision, we still recommend getting advice from a qualified professional. Vault Clerk helps you walk into that conversation prepared, with your paperwork in order.

How it works

From a messy Inbox to a vault you can find things in.

Independent musicians run a small business out of downloads, email attachments and desktop folders. Vault Clerk works like the clerk at a bank vault: it takes what you hand over, puts it in the right box, and brings back what you need when a request comes in.

I

Inbox

Point it at a messy Inbox folder. It suggests where each file belongs in your vault, with a short reason.

  • Smart filing suggestions, each with a reason
  • Deposit a batch, or select all
  • Spots duplicates and old versions
  • Suggests clean names for bounces and stems
  • Reads scans and photos of documents on Mac
II

File

You check the plan and say yes. It copies files into your vault.

  • Then the Inbox originals move to your vault's Trash, unless you keep them
  • Nothing is ever deleted
  • A page for every project
  • Mark each project LIVE, ACTIVE or DARK, with the reason shown
III

Request

Paste a sync brief, live offer or collab request. It builds the package: filled paperwork, the right audio and a rights summary.

  • Reads an email file, screenshot, PDF or voice note
  • Ticks the paperwork a brief needs and pre-fills it from the brief, your project and your checked details. Fees, splits and signatures are left for you
  • Drafts a reply and picks tracks, with each one's rights status
  • Matches by how your songs sound, using GenreMatch reads you save
III

Request, up close

IV

Pitch

Make a private link for one person at share.vaultclerk.com, and send it yourself. Only what you tick goes online. The link closes when you say, or on the date you set.

  • A private link for one person, or a room you host on your own website
  • Pick single tracks and what they can do and see
  • Switch a link off and on until its date
  • DARK projects share previews only

Recorded with the sample vault that comes with the app.

Kits: any template you already use gets its own fill-in box. It never fills in a signature line.

Directory

Know who to pitch.

Your own contacts, plus a starting list of places to pitch, play and get paid. Built into the app, so it works offline.

  • Your contacts. Import from Apple or Google Contacts, or save the sender straight from a brief. They stay on your computer: never uploaded, never sent to any AI, never put in a share link.
  • Venues. Venues in Sydney and Los Angeles to start, each with how it wants to be pitched and a link to its own page.
  • Everything else. Radio, sync companies, ticketing and listing platforms, studios, tools and guides, with their published terms.

GENREMATCH

Where your song finds its crate.

GenreMatch is our genre-reading tool. Inside Vault Clerk it reads the masters you pick and files what it hears with the project, so you can pitch by how your songs sound.

  • Send a master, get a readPick a master in the GenreMatch tab. The read comes back with a genre shelf and the reasons for it, the near misses, the tempo, and sync notes such as moods, energy and the scenes it suits.
  • Filed with the projectAfter you say yes, the read is saved in the project's GenreMatch folder as a card you can open, and listed under Your sound. Remove one any time; it goes to your vault's Trash.
  • Briefs matched by soundWhen a brief asks for a mood or a genre, Request uses your saved reads to suggest tracks, not just their titles.

This tab needs the internet to load GenreMatch. Vault Clerk hands it the track you pick and its project name, nothing else, and asks before saving anything. A read is an estimate of how a song sounds, not a fact about it.

QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

What is Vault Clerk?

Vault Clerk is a desktop app for independent musicians. It suggests where each file in a messy Inbox belongs in your vault, searches inside your documents, and answers briefs such as sync requests and live offers with filled paperwork, the right audio and a rights summary. You approve every step.

Do my files go online?

Only if you make a share link. Then only the files you tick go online, for that link, until you switch it off or its date passes. Everything else stays on your computer.

Does GenreMatch upload my music?

Vault Clerk doesn't upload it. It hands the track you pick to the GenreMatch tab on your computer, and GenreMatch reads it there. The tab needs the internet to load, and nothing is saved to your vault until you say yes.

Will Vault Clerk delete or move my files?

It never deletes. It copies files into your vault, and only after you say yes does it move the Inbox originals to the vault's Trash (or leave them, if you choose). Only you empty the Trash. Duplicates and junk go there too.

Does Vault Clerk use AI?

Filing works mostly from clear rules and how you've filed before, and it learns from your corrections. A local AI model is optional and runs on your own computer. Nothing is sent to a cloud AI, and your identity and tax drawer is never sent to any AI.

Can Vault Clerk send emails or share files for me?

It never emails anyone for you. It builds the package, and you send it yourself. To share audio, make a private link for one person, or export a room to host on your own website.

Which contract templates are included?

18 templates come with the app, in four ready-made looks: Producer Agreement, Session Player Agreement, Split Sheet, Live Performance Agreement, Rider, Stage Plot, Sync & Master Licence, Commissioned Score Agreement, Statement of Work and a one-page NDA, plus a Quote, Invoice, Delivery Receipt, Company Books, Project Expenses, Deal Brief, Project One-Pager and Project Metadata. They are included with the app, not an add-on. You can use your own templates instead.

Is any of this legal or financial advice?

No. Vault Clerk isn't a lawyer, an accountant or a financial adviser, and nothing in the app is legal, tax or financial advice. It keeps your paperwork in order and helps you prepare. Before you sign a contract or make a money decision, we still recommend getting advice from a qualified professional. The templates are starting points; have a lawyer check before you sign.

Are Directory listings paid for?

No. Nobody pays to be listed, and we don't earn from anything in the Directory.

Where do the Directory listings come from?

From each venue's or organisation's own website. Every listing shows the date we checked it, and the list is updated with each app release.

Which computers does Vault Clerk run on?

macOS 10.15 or later on Apple Silicon and Intel Macs, and Windows 10 and 11 (64-bit), in beta.
